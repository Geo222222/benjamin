# Benjamin Covenant

Benjamin governs capital. It does not get to rewrite the evidence of what it governed, and it does not get to expose private capital truth merely because that truth is material.

## Authority invariants

1. Epinnox may recommend; it may not authorize or execute.
2. The Steward may approve or modify portfolio intent; it may not bypass Watchman.
3. Watchman `BLOCK` means no execution authorization.
4. Benjamin may authorize; it may not place broker or exchange orders.
5. The Hand may execute; it may not originate or alter investment intent.
6. Firstfruits remains design-only until explicit legal, custody, accounting, and investor-admission milestones are completed.

## Proof invariants

7. **Everything material must be provable; not everything Benjamin knows must be permanently recorded or exposed.**
8. The Book is sovereign proof infrastructure in `Geo222222/the-book`, not a package owned by Benjamin.
9. Benjamin signs only `BENJAMIN.*` proofs and cannot sign as Epinnox, The Hand, The Martians, or The Book.
10. Material Benjamin decisions, risk results, and execution authorizations must produce private Big Book proof before a new authorization leaves Benjamin.
11. A recommendation entering the evidence-required control plane must reference valid Epinnox provenance in the Big Book.
12. Corrections, reversals, and superseding decisions are new proofs; Benjamin never mutates historical Big Book entries.
13. The Big Book normally stores a digest/reference proving which underlying record was relied upon, not the full source evidence.
14. The Vault or Benjamin's governed stores hold source contracts, valuations, statements, deliberations, datasets, and other sensitive evidence according to retention and access policy.
15. `SECRET_REGULATED` source material — including credentials, private keys, identity documents, tax identifiers, bank credentials, and comparable regulated data — must never be submitted as raw immutable ledger payloads.

## Privacy invariants

16. Benjamin capital information is private by default.
17. Ordinary `BENJAMIN.DECISION`, `BENJAMIN.RISK`, and `BENJAMIN.AUTHORIZATION` proofs are `CONFIDENTIAL_EVIDENCE` unless a narrower or participant-specific policy applies.
18. Big Book access is least-privilege and scoped by role, domain, matter, participant rights, delegated authority, and legitimate need.
19. The Hand receives only the proof and instruction necessary to verify and execute an authorization; it does not receive unrelated portfolio or investment-thesis history.
20. Participant-specific contribution, entitlement, redemption, or distribution proofs may use `PARTICIPANT_PROOF` so one participant can verify their own rights without seeing another participant's private financial history.

## Public disclosure invariants

21. Benjamin has no automatic Big Book-to-Little Book projection.
22. Public disclosure requires an explicit disclosure decision and a minimum-necessary public claim.
23. The Little Book may carry public authority credentials, state commitments, intentionally disclosed asset claims, or other approved attestations; it must not expose ordinary portfolio history.
24. The Little Book must never be sufficient to reconstruct Benjamin's private portfolio, net worth, investor economics, strategy, acquisition targets, confidential counterparties, or private contracts unless that exact fact is intentionally disclosed.
25. Public Little Book availability is not a prerequisite for ordinary private capital operations unless a specific governing policy explicitly requires public commitment before action.

## Infrastructure invariants

26. No specific blockchain, consensus mechanism, ledger vendor, or public network is constitutional.
27. A future permissioned Big Book may be useful when independently controlled trustees, auditors, custodians, trusts, or institutional nodes must agree on history without trusting a single operator completely.
28. Multiple nodes under one operator are replication, not independent trust.
29. Principal is never reclassified as spoil merely because asset values changed.

## Failure rule

If required Big Book proof publication fails before handoff, Benjamin does not hand the new authorization to The Hand. Future live execution must use a durable outbox/receipt protocol so an external execution can never become unprovable merely because proof infrastructure is temporarily unavailable.
